Based on the company website at the time this pack was prepared:
- Title search and examination support, including chain-of-title analysis and draft commitment preparation.
- Title plant indexing, conversion, building and maintenance.
- Property-data normalisation and enrichment.
- MLS, listing and PropTech data workflows.
- Processing of recorded documents and related property data, including deeds, mortgages, assignments, liens, releases, satisfactions, affidavits, assessor/tax records, parcel/GIS data, plat maps and other records.

## 3. How the workflow is described
A useful high-level model:
1. Collect property documents and data from relevant sources.
2. Classify document/instrument types.
3. Extract property, ownership and title-related fields.
4. Normalise and standardise information.
5. Connect related records and property context.
6. Apply confidence scoring, source traceability and validation.
7. Route uncertain or critical fields for human review where configured.
8. Deliver structured outputs into the customer's existing workflows.

This is a simplified explanation, not a guarantee that every customer workflow follows the exact same sequence.

## 6. Core industry problems worth exploring
- County records are fragmented and vary by jurisdiction.
- Documents may be scanned, handwritten, inconsistent or difficult to classify.
- Manual searching, re-keying and review can consume staff time.
- Missing context or weak source traceability makes verification harder.
- Automation must manage uncertainty, exceptions and accountability.
- Title work involves legal and financial risk; AI outputs should support qualified professionals, not be represented as replacing authorised judgment.
- Data access, county digitisation, coverage and record freshness can constrain automation.

## 7. Messaging principles
Lead with customer outcomes and proof, not vague AI hype.
Useful themes:
- From documents to usable property intelligence.
- Faster preparation with evidence still attached.
- Automate repeatable work; focus human review where it matters.
- Make fragmented records easier to search, connect and verify.
- Fit into existing systems rather than forcing a complete workflow replacement.

Avoid unsupported claims such as “100% accurate,” “fully autonomous title search,” “eliminates all fraud,” “replaces examiners,” or guaranteed savings/turnaround.

## 8. Competitor landscape — initial reference
### Titl — https://www.titl.co/
Public positioning: AI-powered title search and automation. Its website describes searching county land records, extracting names/dates/property details/recorded interests, connecting documents, supporting ownership review and linking findings to source records. It serves title agents, underwriters, lenders and governments.
Marketing observations: clear workflow narrative (search → abstract → examine), product UI/process visuals, source-linked evidence, quantified speed/productivity claims and strong “move from records to commitment” framing.
Research caution: verify each performance statistic, geographic coverage, product availability and customer claim before comparison or publication.

### Pippin Title — https://www.pippintitle.com/
Public positioning: nationwide title search provider combining proprietary search technology with a network of human searchers and quality control. Its website describes residential, commercial and energy title searches, order tracking, indexed reports, integrations and its Pip intelligence platform.
Marketing observations: service breadth, turnaround messaging, expert support, nationwide coverage, integrations, testimonials and a direct book-a-demo/order CTA.
Research caution: distinguish Pippin's technology-assisted search and human searcher model from a pure software product. Confirm current details before using.

### First American — https://www.firstam.com/
Public positioning: large title/settlement and property-data business with technology across title production, property information, agent workflows and digital closing. Its public innovation material discusses automation in title search/production, AI, workflow automation and human expertise.
Marketing observations: enterprise trust, broad ecosystem, established distribution, property-data assets, agent tools and end-to-end settlement capabilities.
Research caution: First American is a broad incumbent, not a like-for-like startup competitor in every product category. Compare by workflow and buyer need, not company size alone.

### Competitor analysis rule
Do not label all three as identical direct competitors. Use:
- Direct workflow competitor: overlaps in the exact title-search / document-processing workflow.
- Adjacent competitor: solves a neighbouring workflow or serves the same buyer differently.
- Incumbent/platform competitor: broad data, title or settlement capabilities that may compete in selected use cases.
